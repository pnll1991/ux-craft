# Conflicts, evidence and ethical use

UX principles can point in different directions. Resolve conflicts in this order: safety and informed consent; accessibility and task correctness; explicit project requirements; clarity and recoverability; measured efficiency; visual polish.

A principle is not evidence that a specific implementation works. Distinguish four levels:

- **Observed:** directly visible in code, UI, analytics or a reproduced task.
- **Supported:** backed by a relevant standard, source or measured product data.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible but not yet tested in this product.
- **Unknown:** information required for a strong conclusion is unavailable.

For every important recommendation, record the observed problem, affected task, principle, implementation, trade-off and acceptance check. Do not invent conversion lifts, personas, research findings, accessibility conformance or benchmark results.

Never use cognitive principles to hide costs, create false scarcity, manufacture progress, pressure consent, obstruct cancellation or disguise advertising as interface controls. Optimize for an informed successful task, not for a metric at any cost.
